1. How to read this document

Teaching note Competitive research mixes three kinds of statement. Treating them as equal is the most common way a market slide becomes misleading.
FACT — observed on a vendor or official page ESTIMATE — scenario assumption, not evidence INFERENCE — analyst conclusion from the facts

Unknown means the fetched page did not publish the figure. Accio did not inspect private code; “constraint-based” here means the vendor publicly claims a capacity / deadline / duration solver, not that we reverse-engineered one.

2. One-paragraph verdict

Inference

There is a wedge, but it is not “AI planning.” The defensible problem is: a student has a real timetable, several course deadlines, finite free time, variable energy, and needs one trustworthy answer to “what now, and when can I actually do it?” Academic planners understand classes and exams but do not show a capacity solver. Motion and Reclaim do genuine constraint scheduling, but for professionals. Launch as read-only ICS + module-aware deadlines + deterministic packing + bounded LLM explanation + confirm-before-schedule. Do not lead with “LLM”. Lead with “never miss the work that fits around your actual timetable.”

£0 gravityStudent substitutes are free or ~£15/year
2 solversOnly Motion and Reclaim claim true constraint scheduling
Open jobNo incumbent owns timetable + effort + feasible next block

3. The job to be done

Teaching note The real competitor at 08:00 on Monday is not another app. It is Outlook + LMS + a paper list + last-minute cramming. A product only wins if it is less effort than that fragmented workflow.

Design-centre customer: a full-time Imperial / UK STEM student with 4–8 contact hours some days, an assignment due Tuesday 23:59, plus sport, work or commute.

Six jobs

  1. Capture — one sentence: “revise statistics for two hours tomorrow afternoon.”
  2. Know reality — what is due, how much work remains, is remaining free time enough?
  3. Choose now — given energy, Quick Win or Big Rock?
  4. Place work — a conflict-free block around lectures and the deadline.
  5. Protect life — coursework must not erase career, sport, personal.
  6. Trust — useful suggestions, no invented tasks from every lecture.

Positioning that survives copycats

“The academic workload planner that proves the work fits.” Avoid “AI that plans your life,” which invites ChatGPT and Motion.

Durable: academic data model + solver loop; trust/control as a system; campus distribution. Copyable in six months: NL quick-add, a student discount, an LLM re-ranker on someone else’s scheduler.

5. Price gravity — students do not live in Motion’s world

Teaching note Willingness to pay is anchored by free tools. MyStudyLife’s listed premium is £14.99/year (page still carries a 2024 promo, so treat as unverified current list). Motion Pro AI is $19/seat/month on the annual toggle = $228/year. A student product priced like Motion loses on distribution before it loses on features.

6. What is already built vs still designed

The fusion thesis is: the deterministic engine owns dates, free time, feasibility and packing; the LLM owns parsing, soft judgement, bounded re-ranking and “why.” If the model fails, a rule-based ranker still answers. Recommend, never hijack the calendar.

  • Free-slot search 08:00–22:00, greedy packing, accept/reject
  • In-place personal blocks with TaskCategory colours

Shipped in Ike / designed in fusion

  • Closed suggestion templates, budgets, 24-hour undo
  • Web-first then iOS; no silent mass task generation

How to use this comparison: France is not a “smaller market.” It is a second beachhead with similar headcount and a harder distribution problem (language + fragmented timetable export). Keep the UK as the design centre until (1) ICS/ENT import works for a named French campus cohort and (2) French natural-language capture converts. Then apply the same Free + feasibility paid tier, priced in euros, with campus pilots aimed at student-success / digital-education buyers.

11. Go-to-market beachhead

  1. Imperial design partner — one dense Outlook/ICS programme and a student-success or digital-education contact.
  2. Society-led expansion — STEM, computing, medicine, engineering, taught master’s; ambassadors demo “Monday 08:00”.
  3. Russell Group cluster — portable ICS onboarding, not one connector per university.
  4. UK + Ireland self-serve — then other English-speaking campuses with exportable calendars.
  5. France second beachhead — only after French UI/NL and a proven ENT/ICS import path on one university or école d’ingénieurs cohort (see §8).

Activation funnel to instrument: Import timetable → choose modules → add one real deadline → accept one suggested block → complete or edit it → repeat next day. Product Hunt is the wrong first motion: it reaches productivity enthusiasts, not the timetable/LMS workflow.

12. Kill criteria and the three tests that matter

RiskKill / pause signal
ChatGPT + Calendar already does 80% of the jobMajority will not import a timetable or enter durations after a guided trial
Feed fragmentationValid-event parse rate cannot hit a pre-set beta threshold
Free-tool gravityUsage without paid conversion — keep free; campus licence only with outcomes + sponsor
Wrong recommendationsKill the LLM feature if it does not lift accepted/completed sessions vs deterministic baseline
Fake AI task floodClosed templates, budgets, digest; no mass generation
Academic-year churnDo not count seasonal use as durable retention
Privacy / procurementPause university sales without security evidence; run self-serve in parallel
Motion/Reclaim go down-marketCompete on ontology, distribution, trust — not a permanent feature moat

If we only validate three things next: (1) students will connect a real timetable; (2) a solver-backed suggestion improves completed-before-deadline work versus their current workflow; (3) enough students, or one university budget holder, will pay for that improvement.
